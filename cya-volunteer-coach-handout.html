How We Coach

Every player belongs. Positive and player-centered, all season, every session.

Keep them moving. Dead time is the enemy of a good session.

Praise effort over outcome. Process and attitude beat results at this age.

Be consistent, be patient. Routine and repetition are what build confidence.

Ask, don't tell. "What did you see?" teaches more than "do this."

Fun is the point. If they want to come back next week, you did the job.

01

Game Day, Start To Finish

Format, fields, and what your hour actually looks like.

Read this even if you have coached before. Our U4-U8 game days are not traditional league matches. The format is modified on purpose, by age, to maximize the touches, decisions, and minutes each player gets. If you have experience with older age groups, expect it to look different.

Before you arrive

  • Fields are set up before families arrive. You do not need to move goals or mark lines.
  • Find your assigned field number and be there before your players are.
  • Bring a pump, any first aid supplies provided to you, and extra balls if you have them.
  • No roster to carry at these ages. If you ever need one, it is in Ottosport.
  • Check the forecast and know the weather and lightning policy before you leave home.

Bantams / U4-U6 format

Bantams do not have a separate practice during the week. They have one session, and it is a practice-play format: you run activities with your group first, then they play. Small fields run at the same time so every player is in action at once.

Block 1

Practice

First 25 minutes

Structured games with your full group. Dribbling games, tag games, small competitions. High energy, lots of touches, keep it moving.

Block 2

Play

Next 30 minutes

Players split across small fields and play. U4 and U5 play 2v2, U6 plays 3v3, and there are no goalkeepers at either age.

Block 3

Wrap-Up

Last 5 minutes

Bring everyone together. High fives, one fun recap, send them off with energy.

No traditional restarts at Bantam. No throw-ins, no kick-ins, no corners, no goalkeeper restarts. When the ball leaves the field, a coach or helper feeds a new ball in immediately. Maximum play, zero confusion over rules. Keep it flowing.

Academy / U7-U8 format

Academy game day is small-sided and structured, but it is still a development format. Numbers can flex on the day depending on who shows up and what the group needs. Use your judgement.

Academy

U7

3v3, no goalkeepers

  • Teams split across small fields playing at the same time
  • Halves with a short break
  • Throw-ins and kick-ins apply
  • Subs between halves or at stoppages
Academy

U8

4v4, no goalkeepers

  • Teams split across small fields playing at the same time
  • Halves with a short break
  • Throw-ins and kick-ins apply
  • Subs between halves or at stoppages

Their week. Academy training runs once a week, led by the Director of Rec Soccer and CYA professional staff. That is the session everyone comes to, and it is where the nine-week curriculum lives. Staff coaches demonstrate and help on the field, so you are never inventing a session on the drive over. U8 teams can add a separate practice with their own coach on top of it.

No goalkeepers anywhere through U8. That is deliberate. Every player stays in the field of play and takes part in attacking and defending all game. And even though Academy games feel more like real games, the purpose is still development. Rotate fairly, do not park a player in one position, everyone plays equal time.

Your job on game day

TaskWhat it looks like
ArrivalKnow who showed up and note any injuries or flags. No roster to check off at these ages, and Ottosport has one if you ever need it.
Sideline conductPositive and encouraging. No tactical shouting during play. Your voice should lift players, not steer them.
SubstitutionsRotate consistently. No child sits more than one half. A sub should never feel like a punishment.

How 4 To 8 Year Olds Work

Know this and half the job coaches itself.

Bantams / U4-U6

What you will seeHow to respond
Attention span of two to three minutes per activityRotate every two to three minutes and always have the next one ready
Parallel play. They play alongside teammates, not with themSmall groups only. Everyone has a ball

Managing Bantam players

  • Establish an attention signal early. A whistle pattern, a clap pattern, or a call and response.
  • Kneel down to their level when you talk to them. Physical presence matters at this age.
  • Transition fast between activities. Every second of dead time is lost.
  • Use first names constantly. It builds connection and it refocuses attention.
  • Celebrate everything. Great touch, great try, great hustle, great listening.
  • Expect chaos. It is developmentally normal. Redirect with energy, not frustration.

Academy / U7-U8

What you will seeHow to respond
Beginning to understand rules and structureIntroduce simple ideas like attacking side and defending side
Starting to play with teammates, not just near themEncourage passing and reward it loudly when it happens
Can handle five to eight minute activity windowsGive them a clear goal or challenge inside each activity
Competitive. They care about the scoreKeep score and channel it. Did your team improve from last round?
Can receive and apply one coaching pointOne point per activity, maximum. More than that is noise
Beginning to self-regulate emotionallyHold the line on sportsmanship and praise maturity when you see it

Managing Academy players

  • Set two or three clear expectations at the start. They can hold themselves to them.
  • Small-sided always. Four a side max, everyone involved every minute.
  • One coaching point per activity. Deliver it, reinforce it, move on.
  • Use the freeze sparingly. Stop play, make the point in 30 seconds, restart.
  • Push them to talk to each other. Tell your teammate where you want the ball.
  • After the session, ask what they learned. Their answers will surprise you.
03

Running The Session

The two models we use, then the activities we are going to play tonight.

Model 1: Play, Practice, Play

Start with a game, coach what you actually see, then go back to the game. Players stay active, learning stays in context, and your coaching has visible relevance instead of being a lecture before the fun part.

Phase 1

Play

5 to 8 minutes

Free or lightly guided game. Watch what they can and cannot do. This is where you gather what to work on.

Phase 2

Practice

10 to 15 minutes

One or two game-like activities. Train the thing you just watched them struggle with. Build it up gradually.

Phase 3

Play

5 to 10 minutes

Back to the game so they can use it. Coach from the outside. Encourage, do not interrupt the flow.

Why it works. Kids do not learn by being told, they learn by doing. This keeps players on the ball for as much of the hour as possible while giving you a live picture of what actually needs coaching.

Model 2: Build it up, unopposed to opposed

This is how you structure a single activity inside the Practice phase. Introduce it simply, then add pressure in three steps.

Step 1: Unopposed Technique first

The skill with no defender. Pure focus on doing it correctly. No pressure and no decisions yet.

Dribble through cones. Pass against a wall. Shoot at an empty goal.

Step 2: Semi-opposed Add a decision

A passive or shadow defender. Now they have to read it and choose, but a mistake costs little. Technique will get sloppy. That is expected.

1v1 with a jog-only defender. A defender who can shadow but not tackle.

Step 3: Fully opposed Real pressure

Live defender, full pressure, game conditions. Mistakes happen here and that is where it sticks.

1v1 live. Small-sided game. Match play.

Do not skip to step 3. Players need to feel success at each step before pressure goes on. Going straight to live pressure creates frustration and sloppy habits. The common shortcut is skipping steps 1 and 2 because it is "just rec." Even five unopposed minutes sets the tone and gives your coaching point something to attach to.

Say it simply

Short phrases you call out while they play. Pick two or three per activity and repeat them all session.

"Little touches." "Eyes up." "Stop it, go again." "Find space." "Nice, do it again."

Bantam activities / U4-U6

Bantam U4-U6

Simon Says Dribbling

SetupEvery player has a ball in a 15x15 yard grid. Everyone dribbles around and you call out a body part to stop the ball with. Head, foot, heel, knee.

Coaching pointsClose control, head up while dribbling, stay balanced.

Make it fun"Simon says stop it with your ear." Then ask them, is your ball talking to you?

Bantam U4-U6

Popcorn

SetupEvery player with a ball. You kneel inside a 5x5 cone box. Players bring you the ball, you throw it up and away, and they have to chase it down and bring it back to the popcorn machine.

Coaching pointsControl on the move, sharp turns, spatial awareness.

ProgressionBring it back in hands, then rolling it with hands, then dribbling.

Bantam U4-U6

Shark Attack

SetupPlayers are fish and dribble from one end of the grid to the other. The shark, you or a chosen player, tries to kick balls out of the grid.

Coaching pointsShield with your body, look around, keep the first touch close.

ProgressionAdd a second shark. Anyone who loses their ball becomes a shark. Last fish wins.

Bantam U4-U6

1v1 To Mini Goals

SetupPair players on either side of a small 5 yard goal. Every pair plays at the same time. Rotate pairs every two minutes.

Coaching pointsScoring, defending one on one, deciding under a bit of pressure.

ProgressionLet them play with almost no interruptions. Celebrate goals loudly. Superman versus Batman works every time.

Academy activities / U7-U8

Academy U7-U8

Stop And Go, Turns

SetupEvery player has a ball in a 15x15 yard grid. They dribble freely, and on your signal they stop the ball with the sole of the foot and hold it still and balanced. On "go" they are off again.

Coaching pointsFind the space before you move into it. Head up, small touches, ball close.

ProgressionEvery "go" starts with a turn or a move. Pullback, inside cut, outside hook.

Academy U7-U8

Gates

SetupEight to ten small cone gates across a 20x20 yard grid. A point every time a player dribbles through a gate. Play 60 to 90 seconds, then rest.

Coaching pointsDribbling in different directions, scanning ahead, ball mastery moves.

ProgressionLeft foot only. Add a defender who can close a gate by standing in it. Run it as a team race.

Academy U7-U8

1v1 Counter Attack

SetupOne player attacks a small goal against an active defender. If the defender wins it, they turn and attack the goal at the other end. Reset after each rep and swap roles.

Coaching pointsAttacking, take the defender on and shoot early when there is space. Defending, be patient, do not dive in, transition fast.

ProgressionShrink the grid. Add a five second limit to shoot. Two touch max for the attacker.

Academy U7-U8

2v2 Transition

SetupTwo teams of two in a small grid with a goal at each end. When the defending pair wins the ball they attack the opposite goal straight away. No resets, play is continuous. Rotate every two to three minutes.

Coaching pointsWin it and go. Talk to your partner. When to pass and when to drive.

ProgressionThree seconds to shoot after winning it. Expand to 3v3. Add a neutral who always plays with whoever has the ball.

04

Parents, Problems, And People

The part of coaching that has nothing to do with soccer.

The sideline

  • Set it at the first session: positive cheering only, no coaching or instructing from the sideline.
  • Split the sides clearly. Coaches and players on the field side, families opposite.
  • Your composure under pressure is what players and families take home with them.

When it goes sideways

Conflict on the field

Stop play calmly. Separate players if you need to. Handle it quietly, not publicly, and get back to playing quickly. Long stops let emotion build at this age.

Injury

Player first. Do not move them if you suspect a head, neck, or serious impact. Talk to the parent calmly. Contact the club for anything beyond a minor bump.

Weather and lightning

Stop and move to shelter immediately. Do not wait to see whether it passes. No game is worth the risk. Know the club policy before you arrive.

A player shuts down

Move them out of the situation without making it a scene. Sit beside them, not in front of them. Give them a minute before you talk. Bring in a parent if you need to.

Questions we will talk through tonight

  • What does a good session look like to you?
  • What worries you most about managing this age group?
  • When have you seen a coach give players way too much information at once?
  • What do you do with a player who checks out or acts up?
