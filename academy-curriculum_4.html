CYA Soccer
Academy Curriculum

U7 & U8 Boys & Girls Nine weeks Volunteer coached, staff led

A nine-week technical curriculum for the U7 and U8 Academy division. Volunteer coaches run each session. A staff coach demonstrates first. Players build real skills, and coaches learn the progression they carry into U9 and U10.

What the sessions develop

Focus areas

The technical work is the material. But the sessions are built around a few other things that matter at this age too.

Confidence on the ball

Players who can control and turn the ball ask for it instead of avoiding it.

Playing with teammates

Passing and small-team games move players from playing near each other to actually playing with each other.

Athletic foundation

Running, changing direction, striking, and staying balanced against an opponent. These build basic coordination and movement.

Technique

Repeatable skills that hold up in a game, introduced one at a time.

Staying in the game

Players who succeed and have fun are more likely to keep playing next season.

For coaches

Coaching U7 and U8 players

A few things about this age make the sessions work. This section follows standard US Youth Soccer guidance for these ages.

Keep everyone moving

No lines and no long waits. Get a ball on every player's foot whenever you can manage it.

Short attention spans

Quick instructions, short activities, then let them play. Focus arrives in bursts. Don’t fight the cranky days.

Fun comes first

Players who enjoy it keep playing. Seeing their own improvement is a big part of that, so point it out.

They watch your reaction

Encourage often and correct gently. Never measure a player against the best one on the team.

They play well in pairs

Partner activities suit this age. Set the pairs yourself to keep them balanced.

Expect a wide range

One team can hold a player who seems ten and one who seems six. Challenge each player at their own level.

What U8 players are working toward

Dribbling with both feet and all surfaces. Getting out of trouble. Beating a player. A soft first touch. Shielding the ball. An introduction to passing and shooting. Keep tactics and scorekeeping light. Results are not the point at this age.

These sessions are set at a starting level on purpose. Nothing here should feel too hard for a first-time player or coach. If a group races through an activity, our staff can add challenge on the spot.

The shape of every night

Practice Structure

Every week uses the same four blocks in the same order. The professional coach introduces and demonstrates the technical activity. Volunteers run it from there. That predictability is the point, for the kids and the coaches.

Warm-up game 10 min
A fun movement game to open the day: a relay, obstacle course, or tag. No ball needed to start; we work it in once everyone is moving and smiling.
Technical activity 15 min
The week's one skill, shown by staff and coached in small groups.
Small game 15 min
The skill under light pressure, in a game that keeps everyone involved.
Scrimmage 15 to 20 min
Free play. Minimal coaching. Let them figure it out.

Week by week

Fall 2026 Weekly Practices

Tap any week to open the full session plan and its printable PDF.

Warm-upRelay race10 min

Split into small teams behind a start line. On ‘go’, the first player runs down, around a cone, and back to tag the next. Run round one with no ball, then a second round dribbling a ball down and back. Keep teams small so nobody waits.

Say it simply“Ready, set, go!” · “Run fast, turn tight!” · “Now grab your ball!”

TechnicalBall mastery and cone maze15 min

In small groups, players practice toe taps, sole rolls, and inside-and-outside touches standing in place, then dribble through a short maze of scattered cones and back. Staff demonstrates each touch before the players try it.

Say it simply“Little touches!” · “Both feet!” · “Eyes up!”

Small gameSharks and Minnows15 min

Two or three players are 'sharks' in the middle with no ball. Everyone else is a 'minnow' with a ball and tries to dribble from one side of the grid to the other without a shark kicking their ball out. Lose your ball and you become a shark for the next round.

Say it simply“Keep it close!” · “Find a gap!” · “Change speed!”

ScrimmageSmall-sided play15 to 20 min

Split into even teams and play 3v3 or 4v4 to small goals. Keep coaching light and let them play.

Say it simply“Have fun, keep dribbling!”

Why it matters

Players work with a ball for the entire session. This maximizes touches and cuts standing time. Touches are what build coordination and comfort on the ball at this age. Every week after this one assumes players can already dribble.

How it develops

U7 to U8

More touches at a faster pace and with both feet. Players start to keep the ball moving without looking down at it.

U8 to U10

Close control in tight areas. Shielding the ball. Keeping possession under pressure in the 7v7 game.

Warm-upObstacle course10 min

Build a course: weave through a line of cones, hop over a ‘river’ of laid-down pinnies, spin around a cone, then sprint back. Players go one at a time. A few runs with no ball first, then dribble a ball through the whole course.

Say it simply“Weave and wiggle!” · “Big jump over the river!” · “Spin and go!”

TechnicalPullbacks and stop-go15 min

Everyone dribbles freely inside a grid, no cones needed. On ‘pullback’, every player drags the ball back with the sole of the foot and goes a new way. On ‘stop’, everyone traps the ball dead, then ‘go’ sends them off again.

Say it simply“Pull it back!” · “Stop!” · “Go!”

Small gameGates15 min

Scatter eight to ten small gates (two cones about a yard apart) around the area. Each player has a ball and scores a point every time they dribble through any gate. Count points over 60 seconds, rest, then go again to beat their own score.

Say it simply“Heads up!” · “Find the next gate!” · “Turn quick!”

ScrimmageSmall-sided play15 to 20 min

Even teams, 3v3 or 4v4 to small goals. Light coaching, let them play.

Say it simply“Try a turn out there!”

Why it matters

Turning lets a player keep the ball instead of losing it or clearing it under pressure. Working through scattered gates also gets players looking up. Their eyes come off the ball and onto the open space.

How it develops

U7 to U8

Turn away from light pressure and add a change of speed as they come out of the turn.

U8 to U10

Turns are used to beat a pressing defender and switch play. Scanning before receiving becomes a habit.

Warm-upTails tag10 min

Every player tucks a pinnie into the back of their shorts like a tail. On ‘go’, grab other players’ tails without losing yours. Grab one, drop it, keep playing, count tails at the end. No ball, and keep it inside a marked square.

Say it simply“Grab a tail!” · “Protect yours!” · “Quick feet!”

TechnicalFake and go15 min

Everyone dribbles freely in the grid at the same time. Whenever two players get close, both use a fake, lean one way, push the ball the other, to peel away without touching. It’s fast, fun, and it’s the same move they’ll use on a defender.

Say it simply“Lean, then go!” · “Find space!” · “Keep moving!”

Small game1v1 to small goals15 min

Set two small goals about twelve yards apart. One attacker and one defender start level between them; the attacker tries to beat the defender and dribble through either goal. Rotate a fresh pair in after each turn so nobody waits long.

Say it simply“Be brave!” · “Take them on!” · “Try again!”

ScrimmageSmall-sided play15 to 20 min

Even teams, 3v3 or 4v4 to small goals. Light coaching, let them play.

Say it simply“Take players on!”

Why it matters

1v1 is the foundation of attacking play. The willingness to take a defender on is best built young. Frequent reps in a low-pressure setting let players try a move, lose the ball, and go again without penalty.

How it develops

U7 to U8

Add a second move and a change of speed after beating the first defender, and attack 1v1 in tighter space.

U8 to U10

1v1 becomes a deliberate choice in wide areas. Players learn when to take a defender on and when to release the ball.

Warm-upPartner relay10 min

Pair up. Partners run down and back together, then tag the next pair. In round two they pass a ball back and forth between them the whole way. Keep pairs close so the passes stay short and easy.

Say it simply“Stay with your partner!” · “Pass and go!” · “Short and easy!”

TechnicalPassing at increasing distance15 min

Pairs pass and receive, stepping a yard further apart each round. Staff shows the technique: a firm ankle, contact through the middle of the ball, and enough weight that a partner can control it.

Say it simply“Plant your foot!” · “Use the inside!” · “Right to your friend!”

Small gameKnock the Cone15 min

Stand a target cone (or balance a ball on top of a cone) in the middle of the grid. Two teams spread around the outside and pass to try to knock the target over, resetting it after each hit. Keep score.

Say it simply“Look up first!” · “Pass it firm!” · “Talk to your team!”

ScrimmageSmall-sided play15 to 20 min

Even teams, 3v3 or 4v4 to small goals. Light coaching, let them play.

Say it simply“Find a teammate!”

Why it matters

Passing is the first team skill players learn. It’s the start of playing with teammates instead of alone. The inside-of-foot technique here is the pass they’ll use most at every level.

How it develops

U7 to U8

Pass on the move and with both feet over slightly longer range, and combine a pass with a run to receive it back.

U8 to U10

Weighted, accurate passing to keep possession and build out of the back. Played into space and to a moving teammate.

Warm-upFill the nest10 min

Scatter pinnies or cones across the grid and give each team a hoop or cone as their ‘nest’. On ‘go’, players run out, grab one item at a time, and bring it home. Most items wins. To add the ball, have them trap a gently rolled ball and dribble it back.

Say it simply“One at a time!” · “Run it home!” · “Soft hands!”

TechnicalTrap and race15 min

A server rolls the ball in. The receiver traps it with a good first touch, then races it out to a line before the coach counts to three. Swap roles every few turns.

Say it simply“Trap it!” · “Touch and run!” · “Beat the count!”

Small gameReceive and Score15 min

A server passes to a player waiting at a cone gate. The player controls the ball, dribbles through the gate, and finishes at a goal a few yards beyond it. Rotate the server regularly.

Say it simply“Control it first!” · “Then go to goal!” · “Stay calm!”

ScrimmageSmall-sided play15 to 20 min

Even teams, 3v3 or 4v4 to small goals. Light coaching, let them play.

Say it simply“Good first touch!”

Why it matters

First touch decides whether a player can keep playing or has to chase a poor control. It comes after passing on purpose. Players now have a reason to control the ball, so they can set up the next pass or dribble.

How it develops

U7 to U8

Take the first touch into space, away from where a defender would be.

U8 to U10

First touch into space under pressure. Controlling different speeds and heights. Setting up to play forward.

Warm-upPairs shuttle10 min

Pairs line up together and shuttle to a cone and back as a two, then high-five the next pair. In round two they carry one ball between them and pass it back and forth on the way. Starting as a pair sets up today’s teamwork.

Say it simply“Together, together!” · “Pass to your buddy!” · “Nice and easy!”

Technical1v1 from a coach pass15 min

The coach passes the ball in to a player, who is now 1v1 against a single defender and attacks a small goal. Simple and direct, no decisions to think through first.

Say it simply“First touch forward!” · “Take them on!” · “Shoot!”

Small game3v315 min

Play 3v3 to two small goals in a compact area. Let the passing and dribbling happen on its own. Keep it flowing with subs ready to swap in.

Say it simply“Pass or dribble, you choose!” · “Great pass!” · “Have fun!”

ScrimmageSmall-sided play15 to 20 min

Even teams, 3v3 or 4v4 to small goals. Light coaching, let them play.

Say it simply“Pass or dribble, your call!”

Why it matters

Attacking straight off a pass ties together the touch, the dribble, and the finish. The small-sided game then adds a teammate, so players start choosing between dribbling and passing on their own.

How it develops

U7 to U8

Take the coach’s pass in stride and attack 1v1 without slowing down first.

U8 to U10

Combination play in the wider game. More players, less time, and proper support angles.

Warm-upRob the nest10 min

Pile a bunch of balls in the middle and give each team a goal or ‘home’. On ‘go’, players run to the pile, grab one ball, and dribble it home, one at a time, until the pile is empty. Loud, fast, and it finishes everyone near the goals.

Say it simply“Grab one, bring it home!” · “Dribble, don’t carry!” · “Go, go, go!”

TechnicalStriking technique15 min

Demonstrate the shot: plant the non-kicking foot beside the ball pointing at the target, strike through it with the laces (the top of the foot, not the toe), and follow through toward goal. Players dribble then shoot, one at a time with a coach watching.

Say it simply“Foot next to the ball!” · “Laces, not toes!” · “Aim for the corner!”

Small game1v1 to a goal, or rapid fire15 min

Either play 1v1 to a single goal with a keeper, or run a rapid-fire drill where balls are fed in one after another for quick finishes. Keep the pace high and celebrate the goals.

Say it simply“Shoot early!” · “Follow your shot!” · “Great try!”

ScrimmageSmall-sided play15 to 20 min

Even teams, 3v3 or 4v4 to small goals. Light coaching, let them play.

Say it simply“Take your shot!”

Why it matters

Finishing comes once players can dribble, pass, and receive well enough to create a chance. Striking technique, the plant foot, contact, and follow-through, is its own skill. It needs its own focused reps.

How it develops

U7 to U8

Shoot on the move and from a pass. Use both the laces and the inside of the foot. Aim for the corners.

U8 to U10

Finish under pressure from a closing defender and a real keeper, including first-time finishes.

Warm-upShield your tail10 min

Tails again: a pinnie tucked in the back like a tail. This time the job is to protect yours. Use your body, turn away, and don’t let chasers grab it, while you try to steal theirs. It’s shielding without a ball, and it leads right into today’s skill.

Say it simply“Body in the way!” · “Turn and hide it!” · “Back to the chaser!”

TechnicalShielding15 min

Teach players to protect the ball with their body: get side-on, arm out for balance, and keep the ball on the foot furthest from the defender. A partner applies light pressure while the player shields for a few seconds, then rolls the ball away and dribbles out.

Say it simply“Body in the way!” · “Ball on the far foot!” · “Big and strong!”

Small gameKeep it, then get out15 min

1v1 in a small square with an escape gate on each side. The player with the ball shields it for a few seconds, then tries to dribble out through either gate. The other player tries to win it back, staying on their feet. Swap and rotate often.

Say it simply“Shield it!” · “Now turn out!” · “Stay on your feet!”

ScrimmageSmall-sided play15 to 20 min

Even teams, 3v3 or 4v4 to small goals. Light coaching, let them play.

Say it simply“Protect your ball!”

Why it matters

Shielding is how a young player keeps the ball when someone gets close, instead of panicking and kicking it away. The same game gives a gentle first taste of winning the ball back too, kept light and always on the feet.

How it develops

U7 to U8

Shield the ball a little longer under real pressure, and roll it to the far foot to turn out.

U8 to U10

Shielding buys time to lay the ball off. The light defending here grows into pressuring on the feet, without diving in.

Warm-upTeam’s choice10 min

Let the players vote on their favorite warm-up from the season, relay, obstacle course, or tails tag, and run it one last time. It sets a fun, relaxed tone for the final day.

Say it simply“You pick today!” · “Let’s have fun!” · “One more time!”

TechnicalSkills circuit15 min

Set up a short, relaxed circuit of stations that revisit the season's moves: a dribble maze, a turn, a pass, and a shot. Keep it light and fun rather than a test.

Say it simply“Show me your moves!” · “Try them all!” · “Have fun!”

Small gameWorld Cup15 min

Run a mini-tournament: split into small teams, let each pick a country, and play a series of short 3v3 or 4v4 games to small goals. Keep each game brief so everyone plays a lot.

Say it simply“Play for your team!” · “Great teamwork!” · “Everyone plays!”

ScrimmageFree play and recognition15 to 20 min

Finish with open small-sided play, then hand out simple recognition such as most improved or best teammate.

Say it simply“Just play and have fun!”

Why it matters

Small-sided games let players apply the season’s skills without direction from the sideline. Coaching backs off in the final week on purpose. It shows what players have actually retained, and gives them room to make their own decisions.

How it develops

U7 to U8

More deliberate decisions in small-sided games, with a basic sense of spacing and positions.

U8 to U10

The full 7v7 game. Holding shape. Making decisions without being told what to do.

Notes for coaches

For the coaching staff

One progression across nine weeks

The sequence is deliberate. It starts with the individual and the ball. Turning and 1v1 come next. Then passing, receiving, and decision-making layer on top. Defending and finishing come once players have the control to use them. The final weeks move into game play.

Running all nine weeks follows the same progression used at U9 and U10. The how it develops note on each week shows where the skill goes next, at U8 and again at U10. Staff demonstrates every activity first. Coaches take it from there.

At this age, coach mainly through the activities and games. Keep instructions short. Give players as many repetitions as possible. Let the small-sided games do most of the teaching.
